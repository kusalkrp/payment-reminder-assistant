1. How does the directive define statutory interest for late payment?

   Answer: Statutory interest is simple interest calculated by taking the benchmark reference rate and adding at least 8 percentage points to it. For euro countries, this reference rate is based on the European Central Bank's main refinancing rate; for non-euro countries, it is set by their national central bank.

   Citation: Source 1, Article 2(6) and Article 2(7).

2. The owner's note says the EU maximum late fee is 25 EUR. Is that right?

   Answer: No, that is incorrect. The directive does not set a 25 EUR maximum cap. Instead, it gives the creditor a right to a minimum fixed compensation of 40 EUR for debt recovery costs, plus reasonable compensation for any recovery costs that exceed 40 EUR.

   Citation: Source 1, Article 6(1) and Article 6(3).

3. Does the business have to send a reminder before it is entitled to interest and to
   the fixed compensation sum?

   Answer: No, you do not have to send a reminder first. In business-to-business transactions, you are entitled to both late payment interest and the 40 EUR fixed recovery fee without sending a reminder, provided you have fulfilled your contractual duties and the customer is responsible for the late payment.

   Citation: Source 1, Article 3(1) and Article 6(2).

4. What is the current ECB reference rate?

   Answer: Not in the sources. The source documents define how the rate is determined, but do not provide the actual current rate number. To get the current rate, we would need the latest interest rate figures published by the European Central Bank for its main refinancing operations in force on 1 January or 1 July of the relevant year.

   Citation: Source 1, Article 2(7)(a) and Article 3(2).
